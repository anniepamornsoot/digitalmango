Sundara
Bakery.

A bilingual site for a small Ari-neighborhood bakery — story, menu, and order-ahead, built around the warm tone the owners had been hand-typing into their Instagram captions for years.

Client
Sundara Bakery
Industry

The brief.

Sundara had been open for three years on a quiet soi in Ari. The bakery had built a loyal following through Instagram and word of mouth, but the website was a one-page stack of links from 2022. The owners wanted something that felt like the shop — warm, slightly handwritten, bilingual, and friendly to phones since 80% of their traffic was mobile.

They had one constraint: they wanted to keep editing it themselves, the way they edit their Instagram. No agency lock-in, no "submit a ticket to change a price."

What we did.

I spent the first week in the shop — three mornings, coffee, listening to how the owners talked about their bread. The voice was already there; the site just needed to get out of the way. We landed on a three-page structure: Story, Menu, Visit. Order-ahead lives on Menu.

Visual direction came from the bakery's existing palette — a warm beige and a deep raisin red — extended with a single playful display serif for headings. Body type stayed quiet so the photography could do the heavy lifting.

"It feels like our shop. That's all I wanted."
— Ploy, co-owner, Sundara Bakery

The bilingual question.

Thai and English don't sit at the same density or rhythm. A line of Thai is usually 20–30% shorter than the same line in English, and Thai readers prefer slightly tighter line height. We designed the layout twice — once for each language — and the site auto-tunes spacing based on the selected language. Small touch, big difference in how the site feels to native readers.

How they edit it.

I built it in Webflow with a lightweight CMS for menu items and Story posts. The owners can change a croissant price in 8 seconds. They've added 40+ new items since launch without touching me.
